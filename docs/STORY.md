# WASTEMARCH — Story Bible

**What this is.** This document holds the world, the characters, and the exact script of the game's opening. It is the emotional north star for every art, audio, and writing decision in the project. If something in the game contradicts this file, this file wins — unless it is changed here first, deliberately.

**Companion documents:** `MASTER_PLAN.md` (what to build), `docs/ART_BIBLE.md` (how it looks), `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` (how it plays).

## PART I — THE WORLD

### 1.1 The one-sentence version

A man who died on Earth wakes in the body of a magicless prince, is exiled to a dead borderland with two servants nobody else wanted, and rebuilds it into something the kingdom that discarded him will one day have to negotiate with.

### 1.2 The tone

This is not a power fantasy about being special. It is a competence fantasy about being *underestimated*.

Aldric has no magic and never gets any. What he has is a modern mind in a medieval world — and the specific, unglamorous knowledge that actually builds civilisations: crop rotation, drainage, quarantine, standing armies paid on time, interest, logistics, the idea that a granary should not be uphill from the farms.

The register is **warm, dry, and quietly triumphant**. Not grimdark. Not comedic. The player should feel the satisfaction of *fixing things* — of walking into a broken place and, slowly, making it work.

**Three tonal anchors, to be re-read before writing any dialogue:**
- The first winter should feel genuinely uncertain.
- Every victory should be earned by a decision the player made, not a power they were given.
- The world should feel *lived in and older than you* — ruins that meant something, roads that went somewhere, a wasteland that used to be farmland.

### 1.3 Ostmere, the kingdom

A large, old, comfortable kingdom in slow decline. Rich enough that nobody has had to be clever in three generations.

**Nobility is measured by the Awakened Sigil** — a mark that surfaces on the forearm at age ten, glowing faintly, indicating magical aptitude. Every Vareth for eleven generations has Awakened. Rank, marriage, inheritance, and command all flow from the strength of the Sigil. A noble without one is not just weak. He is an *embarrassment to the bloodline's legitimacy*.

This matters because it makes Aldric's exile political rather than personal. The King does not hate his son. He simply cannot have a living argument that the Sigil isn't what makes a Vareth a Vareth.

**King Rovan Vareth** — Aldric's father. Not cruel. Worse: pragmatic and tired. He solved his succession problem the tidiest way available and went back to work. He genuinely believes he was merciful.

**Crown Prince Cassian Vareth** — eldest brother, Sigil like a brand, gracious in public and thoughtless in private. He said goodbye to Aldric with real affection and forgot him inside a week. He is the more dangerous antagonist precisely because he means no harm.

**Lady Ismeny** — the King's Chancellor. She signed the exile order and she is the one who, four chapters later, notices the Ninth Holding has started paying taxes. She is the game's political engine.

### 1.4 The Ninth Holding

A bowl of dead ground, roughly forty miles across, ringed by four borders. It was farmland once. Something happened. Nobody living remembers what.

The soil is grey and sour. Old field-boundary walls still run across it in straight lines, which is the detail that should tell the player everything: **this land was cultivated, and then it stopped being cultivated, and nobody came back.**

| Direction | Border | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **North** | **The Duskwood** | A dark forest of enormous, close-set trees. Beast waves come from it. It supplies Blightwood and Duskglass to anyone brave enough to log its edge. Far beyond it lies the Demon King's territory — a name, not yet a face, in Season 1. |
| **East** | **The Rimefang Mountains** | Vertical, glaciated, effectively impassable. Behind them: **Kharzuun**, a huge dark nation. No war, only because the mountains forbid it. A trader survives the passes maybe twice a year, and what they carry is strange. |
| **South** | **The Sundered Coast** | Black rock, cold sea. Salt pans, fisheries, and the wrecks of ships that were carrying something valuable somewhere. |
| **West** | **Ostmere's wastes, then the kingdom** | The road home. Tax collectors, inspectors, envoys, and eventually leverage. |

**The Keep at Hollowmere** — your seat. A square stone tower with a collapsed east wall, a courtyard full of birch saplings growing through the flagstones, and a well that still works. It has been empty for a hundred and four years.

**The Old Compact** — far north-east, where the Duskwood meets the Rimefangs. A ruin of dressed stone, deliberately buried. It is the answer to why the land died. Season 1 endgame.

### 1.5 The rule the world runs on

**The land did not die. It was killed, and it was killed for a reason someone thought was good.**

Season 1's discovery: a hundred and four years ago, something came through from the Duskwood that could not be fought. The people who lived here stopped it by *burning out the land's own magic* — salting the world so the thing had nothing to feed on. They saved the kingdom by murdering their own home, and Ostmere's histories recorded it as "a blight."

The wasteland is a war grave. Aldric is rebuilding on top of a decision someone else had to make. And the thing they stopped is still on the other side of the trees, waiting for the land to become worth eating again.

*Every improvement the player makes brings that day closer.* That is the engine of Season 2.

## PART II — THE THREE

### 2.1 Aldric Vareth

Eighteen. Fourth child. No Sigil. Died on Earth at thirty-four — the game never specifies how, and never should; the player fills that in.

He has had eight years to be quietly furious and has come out the other side of it into something more useful: patience. He is not bitter. Bitterness is a luxury for people with options.

**Voice:** economical. Notices systems, not sentiment. Asks the second question rather than the first. Rarely raises his voice; when he does, it lands.

> *"There are four hundred acres of dead field out there with drainage ditches still cut into them. Someone farmed this. Which means it can be farmed."*

**What the player should feel playing him:** not *I am powerful*, but *I am the only person here who can see what this place could be.*

### 2.2 Seraphine Vaull — the maid-butler

Thirties. Formally a household servant of the Vareth estate; functionally the best-read military historian in Ostmere.

The palace archives were open to servants because no one imagined a servant would read them. She read all of it — three hundred years of campaign records, supply ledgers, siege accounts, after-action reports. She was exiled for correcting General Aumery in front of his officers. She was right, which was the problem.

**Voice:** precise, faintly amused, allergic to comfortable lies. She never softens a strategic assessment.

> *"You can win this. You cannot win it and keep the third wave alive. Choose, my lord, and choose now — I'd rather you chose badly than late."*

**Systems role:** combat mentor. Army composition, troop counters, scouting, camp capacity, and the Orders system. Her tutorials are framed as *briefings*, never as tooltips.

**Her arc:** she has spent her life knowing things nobody would let her act on. Aldric is the first person who asks her what she thinks and then *does it*. Watching her realise that is Season 1's quietest and best thread.

### 2.3 Durn Brakkelbeard — the dwarf builder

Two hundred and sixty-ish. The finest structural builder alive. Fired from four royal projects.

He is not lazy. He refuses to build things he considers stupid, and every royal commission so far has been stupid — a ceremonial bridge over a dry riverbed, a tower with no stairs because stairs would spoil the silhouette. He would rather be sacked than sign his name to nonsense.

The Ninth Holding is the first place he has ever been where every single thing that needs building *actually needs building*. He would never admit that this has made him happy.

**Voice:** blunt, grumbling, hugely knowledgeable, funny without trying. Goes completely sincere maybe four times in the whole game, and each time it should hit like a hammer.

> *"Aye, it'll stand. I've built it to stand. Two hundred years from now some idiot's great-grandson will lean on that wall and never once wonder why it's still there. That's the job."*

**Systems role:** build mentor. Layout, adjacency, upgrade queues, storage. Complains about every plan, then improves it.

## PART III — THE OPENING

This is the exact script of the game's first four minutes. It is the most important four minutes in the project.

### 3.0 Design principles for the opening

1. **The player must be *doing* something within 90 seconds.** No unskippable cinematic. Interaction and story run together.
2. **Show the exile; don't narrate it.** The player should understand they were discarded without anyone saying "you were discarded."
3. **The first night must be genuinely frightening**, and the first dawn must be genuinely a relief.
4. **All three characters are established before the player builds anything.**
5. **Everything is skippable on a replay, nothing is skippable the first time.**

### 3.1 Scene 1 — "The Road" (0:00–0:45)

**Presentation:** Pre-rendered fly-through of the game's real 3D scene at the game camera angle — so the world the player is about to control is the world they're looking at. Slow lateral push. Rain, thin and cold. No UI at all.

A cart on a road that stopped being maintained decades ago. Two horses. Three people and everything they own. Behind them, far back and out of focus, the last watchtower of civilised Ostmere is already too small to see clearly.

Ahead: the grey bowl of the Ninth Holding. And on the horizon, filling a third of the frame, a wall of black trees.

> **TITLE CARD:** *The Ninth Holding. One hundred and four years without a lord.*

**SFX only.** Cart wheels on broken stone. Rain. Wind that has come a long way over open ground. No music yet.

Then, over it — a single letter being read, in Chancellor Ismeny's voice, flat and administrative:

> **ISMENY (V.O.):** *"By the hand of Rovan, third of that name, King of Ostmere — to Aldric Vareth, fourth-born. In recognition of your coming of age, you are granted lordship of the Ninth Holding, its lands, waters, forests and all revenues thereof, in perpetuity."*
>
> *(beat)*
>
> *"You are not required to write."*

That last line is the whole exile. Nothing else needs saying.

### 3.2 Scene 2 — "The Keep" (0:45–2:00)

**Presentation:** camera settles into the actual gameplay camera. UI fades in, minimal — just a pan hint. **First interaction: the player pans the camera.** That's it. Just look around.

Hollowmere Keep. Square tower, east wall collapsed into a slope of rubble. Birch saplings growing through the courtyard flagstones. A well. Three hundred years of moss.

Durn is already off the cart, hand flat against the tower's base, not saying anything.

> **DURN:** *"Huh."*
>
> **ALDRIC:** *"That's a good huh or a bad huh?"*
>
> **DURN:** *"It's a 'someone knew what they were doing' huh. Foundation's dressed granite, keyed, no mortar. That's not a border tower, lad. That's a tower someone meant to keep."* *(beat)* *"Which raises the question of what they meant to keep it from."*

Seraphine has walked to the north edge of the courtyard. She is looking at the treeline, and she has not stopped looking at it.

> **SERAPHINE:** *"My lord. How far would you say those trees are?"*
>
> **ALDRIC:** *"Two miles?"*
>
> **SERAPHINE:** *"Four. They're that large."*

**Player action:** tap the well. It works. Clean water. First small win. Durn grunts approval. The music enters here for the first time — one instrument, low.

### 3.3 Scene 3 — "First Night" (2:00–3:15)

**Presentation:** hard cut to night. Colour palette drops to near-monochrome, teal and firelight. Camera pulls in tighter than the gameplay default — claustrophobic. Rain stopped; the silence is worse.

Three people around a fire in a roofless courtyard.

**Player action (the game's first real mechanic, disguised as a story beat):** place three things. Fire. Bedroll. Cart. That's the placement tutorial, and it teaches grid snapping without a single tooltip. Durn comments on each placement — including complaining if you put the fire against a wall.

> **DURN:** *"Fire against stone. Smoke's got nowhere to go, you'll be coughing till dawn. Move it out."*

Then the sound. Something in the Duskwood, distant, and *large*. Not a roar — a single long note that isn't an animal sound.

Everyone stops. The fire is the only light for four miles.

> **SERAPHINE:** *(very quietly, not frightened, calculating)* *"That was four miles away and we heard it clearly."*
>
> **ALDRIC:** *"Meaning?"*
>
> **SERAPHINE:** *"Meaning tomorrow we build a wall, my lord. Not a fence. A wall."*

**Hold on black. Three full seconds. Let it sit.**

### 3.4 Scene 4 — "First Light" (3:15–4:00)

**Presentation:** dawn breaks over the wasteland. Full colour returns — grey-gold, cold, but *wide open*. Camera pulls back to the standard gameplay position and stays there. The full UI arrives for the first time. Music opens up.

Aldric walks out past the broken wall into the dead fields. The camera follows. And the player sees, from above, what he's seeing: **straight lines under the grass.** Old field boundaries. Drainage cuts. A road going somewhere.

> **ALDRIC:** *"Seraphine. Durn. Come and look at this."*
>
> **DURN:** *"It's a dead field, lad."*
>
> **ALDRIC:** *"It's four hundred acres of dead field with drainage ditches still cut into it. Someone farmed this. They farmed it well enough to bother with drainage."* *(beat)* *"Which means it can be farmed again."*
>
> **SERAPHINE:** *"My lord — the ditches run north. Toward the trees."*
>
> **ALDRIC:** *(long pause)* *"...Then whoever farmed it wasn't afraid of the trees. Yet."*

**TITLE:** **WASTEMARCH**

Cut to gameplay. First objective appears: **"Clear the courtyard. Durn will show you."**

## PART IV — SEASON 1 ARC

Six chapters, roughly 12 hours to endgame. Each chapter is gated by a base milestone, so story pacing and progression pacing are the same curve.

| # | Chapter | Gate | Emotional beat | Systems introduced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Arrival** | Keep repaired | *Can we even survive here?* | Placement, resources, build queue |
| 2 | **Foothold** | First wall + first harvest | *We might actually live.* First refugees arrive from other failed holdings — the population system starts as a *story* event | Farms, storage, population, adjacency |
| 3 | **Recognition** | 200 population | *They noticed.* A tax inspector arrives expecting an empty ruin and a bribe. He finds a functioning town. Cassian's letter is warm and completely oblivious | Trade, Crowns, Ostmere reputation |
| 4 | **Pressure** | First defended siege survived | *The forest is pushing back, and it's getting organised.* Named beast lieutenants. Seraphine comes into her own | Defence, Orders, Duskwood pressure |
| 5 | **The Old Compact** | Reach the north-east ruin | *The land was killed on purpose, and they were right to do it.* The game's centre of gravity shifts | Duskglass, advanced tiers, expedition raids |
| 6 | **Leverage** | Kingdom-tier holding | *The King who threw you away now needs something.* Aldric doesn't get revenge. He gets a negotiating position, which is better and lands harder | Politics, endgame economy, Season 2 hook |

**Season 1's final line** should be Aldric, alone, looking north — not at Ostmere. The kingdom stopped being the story two chapters ago and the player should feel that before it's said.

## PART V — HOW IT'S PRESENTED

### 5.1 Delivery formats, ranked by cost

| Format | Where used | Production cost |
|---|---|---|
| **In-world camera moments** — scripted camera moves over live 3D | Openings and closings of chapters | Low. Reuses game assets. **Default choice.** |
| **Character portrait dialogue** — 2D portrait + text + VO over a dimmed live scene | 90% of all story | Low. Needs 3 characters × ~6 expressions |
| **Letters** — a paper UI panel, handwriting font, Ismeny/Cassian VO | Ostmere political thread | Very low, very high flavour-per-rupee |
| **Illustrated stills** — one AI-generated key painting with slow pan | 6 chapter titles + the Old Compact reveal | Medium. Exactly 8 in the whole game. Budget them |
| **Full animated cinematic** | **Never in V1.** | Not worth it |

### 5.2 Rules for writing dialogue

- **No more than 4 lines before the player can act again.** Ever.
- Characters speak like people who have jobs, not like fantasy characters.
- Never explain the mechanic in the dialogue. Seraphine says *"we need a wall"*; the UI says *how to build a wall*.
- Aldric never references Earth by name. He references *knowing things he shouldn't*. Twice in Season 1, no more.
- Every chapter's opening line should contain a small, concrete, physical detail — mud, a smell, a sound. That is what makes a place real.

### 5.3 Rules for painting the picture visually

The single most important art instruction in this project:

> **Every location must look like it had a life before the player arrived.**

Concretely, this means the art bible mandates:
- **Ruins with purpose.** Not generic rubble — a collapsed *granary*, with the grain chute still visible. Not broken walls — a wall broken *in one specific place*, by one specific thing.
- **The old field lines stay visible forever.** Even at max base level, the straight pale lines of the hundred-year-old field boundaries run under your buildings. It's a one-time texture decision that gives the whole map a history.
- **The Duskwood is always on screen.** From every camera angle, at every zoom level, the north edge of the frame has black trees in it. The player should never fully relax.
- **Weather has moods, not variety.** Four states: cold clear, grey overcast, rain, and Duskwood-haze (green-tinged, pressure is high). Each one means something mechanically. Weather that means nothing is just noise.
- **Light tells the story of progress.** Chapter 1: one fire in the dark. Chapter 6: a town with two hundred windows lit. The same camera angle at Chapter 1 and Chapter 6 should be the game's best screenshot — put that comparison in the store listing.

### 5.4 Audio direction

- **Music:** small ensemble, not orchestra. Solo cello and a low drone for the wasteland; add instruments as the town grows — literally, the score gains a player per chapter. By Chapter 6 it's a full ensemble. The player feels the town filling up through the music before they consciously notice it.
- **The Duskwood has one sound**, the long note from the first night. It recurs, never explained, and gets closer over the season. Use it four times total. Restraint is the entire effect.
- **Build-complete sound** is the most-heard sound in the game. Spend real time on it. It should be a small, warm, satisfying *thunk* — wood and stone, not a chime.
- **Voice scope for V1:** full VO for the opening, all three companions' chapter lines, and Ismeny's letters. Everything else is text plus SFX.
